I work at the intersection of computational physics, materials science, energy storage, and physics education.

My research interests include sodium-ion batteries, lithium-ion batteries, supercapacitors, two-dimensional materials, and atomistic simulations using density functional theory (DFT).

I also develop physics learning resources and computational approaches that connect fundamental physics with modern materials research.

RESEARCH

Research Directions

Computational approaches for understanding materials, interfaces and energy-storage mechanisms.

01

Na-ion Batteries

Adsorption, diffusion, electronic structure and interfacial interactions of sodium-containing species on advanced electrode materials.

02

Li-ion Batteries

Atomistic understanding of cathode and anode materials, defects, stability and ion-storage behavior.

03

2D Materials

Graphene, TMDs, MXenes and heterostructures for tunable electronic and energy-storage applications.

04

DFT & VASP

First-principles calculations of adsorption energy, charge transfer, ELF, band structures and related properties.

05

Supercapacitors

Computational investigation of electrode materials and ion interactions relevant to high-power energy storage.

06

Computational Physics

Numerical modeling, data analysis and visualization for modern physics and materials research.
